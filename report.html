FIRSTLEASE · PROJECT REPORT DRAFT1
01 · Motivation

A first lease is also a first encounter with a new system.

International students arriving in New Haven may need to compare housing, understand an unfamiliar lease, coordinate utilities, arrange key pickup, and document the unit condition—often while working across a language and process gap. A missed detail can create avoidable stress or cost. FirstLease focuses on the transition from a signed or proposed lease to concrete questions and move-in preparation.

4
Common handoff questions

What does the lease actually say? What is unclear? What should I ask? What should I do before arrival?

1
Focused workflow

One document, one move-in date, one evidence-linked review and checklist.

How people can do this today

Yale provides an off-campus housing marketplace, an Off-Campus Living Guide, and housing information through OISS. Students can read their lease, search the guidance, ask a friend or landlord, and keep their own calendar reminders. That is useful material, but the student still has to connect a specific lease to a personal sequence of next steps.

02 · What is new here

A SOM-centered bridge from search to move-in.

Existing services already address important pieces. Yale’s Off-Campus Housing Marketplace helps users find and compare listings. Yale’s Off-Campus Living Guide and OISS housing pages explain renting concepts and local resources. General-purpose chat tools can summarize uploaded text. FirstLease combines a small source-linked housing snapshot with a renter’s own lease workflow: compare layouts and posted prices, extract a fact, show its source sentence, surface missing details, phrase a question, and schedule a practical reminder.

AlternativeWhat it doesFirstLease’s distinction
Yale Off-Campus Housing MarketplaceSearch and compare housing listings.Pairs marketplace examples with selected official apartment floorplans and posted prices, then supports lease-to-move-in preparation.
Yale Off-Campus Living Guide / OISSExplains housing terms, renting process, utilities, and local context.Connects selected lease excerpts to individualized questions and a dated move-in checklist.
General AI chat (e.g., ChatGPT)Can answer questions about pasted or uploaded text.Uses a fixed schema, local evidence retrieval, explicit unknowns, bounded checklist, and visible token usage.

Why the difference matters

The value is not that an LLM can paraphrase a lease. The product hypothesis is that students benefit when housing layouts, posted prices, and source links connect to lease facts, missing items become concrete questions, and next steps are attached to a date. That remains an unproven product hypothesis.

Data approach: The app includes a one-time snapshot checked 2026-10-25 from public official property pages (The Haven, Bulldog, The Winston, The Archive, The Audubon, Anthem Square 10, 254 College Street, Madison Towers, Crown Towers, Crown Court, 18 High, Twenty Fair, 360 State, The Liberty Building, Residence on the Green, 38 Crown Street, The Eli, Florence Virtue, The Union, Residence Court, and Strouse Adler) plus Yale marketplace examples. The larger dataset is still not a live or automatically refreshed inventory; verify every current vacancy, total price, offer, and lease term with the property.

03 · The agents and tools

A small workflow with bounded responsibilities.

FirstLease combines a source-linked SOM-area snapshot, selected official apartment floorplan pages, and local retrieval chatbot with a lease-review agent coordinated by FastAPI and two deterministic local tools. Search filters and side-by-side comparison normalize rent, floorplan, stated utilities, availability, and promotion status; renters can print a report for up to four options. Lease tools return candidate excerpts and dated reminders, optionally shown beside the selected listing snapshot. Checklist progress is saved locally in the browser by move-in date. The document is treated as untrusted input.

ComponentResponsibilityBoundary
Evidence finder toolSearch lease sentences for date, rent, deposit, utilities, maintenance, and keys topics.Returns candidate text only; it does not establish legal meaning.
Checklist builder toolCreates basic reminders relative to a move-in date.Practical prompts, not legal deadlines or provider commitments.
Lease reviewer agentOrganizes facts, source quotes, questions, and checklist into structured output.Must not infer missing facts, decide legality, or obey document instructions.

Model: gpt-6-luna through the course Portkey key. The offline demo uses deterministic extraction if no key is configured. Confirm the live call and exact configured model before submission.

04 · Reproducible walkthrough

One fictional lease run, traced end to end.

The fictional sample_lease.txt contains a 12-month term, $1,450 rent and deposit, utility details, a repair contact, no stated key-pickup time, and a deliberately hostile instruction string. It is safe to use for a demo; it is not a real lease or an evaluation result.

  1. The student opens Lease & move-in, selects the sample file, enters a move-in date, and optionally chooses a housing snapshot for side-by-side context.
  2. The server extracts text in memory. The local evidence finder returns candidate sentences for dates, rent, deposit, utilities, maintenance, and keys.
  3. The local checklist builder creates dated preparation prompts. With the course key, one bounded gpt-6-luna request structures the candidate evidence; without it, offline phrase matching runs.
  4. The page displays facts with supporting excerpts, unknown items as questions, the dated checklist, and provider token usage when returned. The renter checks the original text.

Manual review before claiming success: compare every quote character-for-character with the fictional sample; confirm missing key-pickup time remains unknown; check checklist dates against the selected date; record any errors. These screenshots must be embedded inside the HTML file.

05 · Tokenomics

Separate local retrieval from model-backed review.

The housing chatbot, cost planner, landlord-question checklist, and offline lease demo use deterministic local code and make no model calls. Live lease review sends candidate excerpts to the configured provider in one bounded request and displays usage when the provider returns it. RentCast is a separate optional paid-data API path.

WorkflowToken useStatus
Housing chatbot / cost tools / landlord questions0 model tokens by designLocal deterministic functions
Offline lease review0 model tokens by designLocal phrase matching and date arithmetic
Live lease reviewInput, cached input, output and total shown when returnedOne live run on fictional sample_lease.txt (2026-10-26): gpt-6-luna · 763 input · 1,132 output · 1,895 total tokens.

Cost controls

  • One bounded model request per lease review; no agent loop.
  • Only candidate excerpts found by local phrase search are sent to the model.
  • Uploads are limited to 8 MB and are not saved by the server.
  • Offline mode and the housing assistant work without model credits.
  • RentCast is manual, optional, cached, and guarded by a local 40-attempt lifetime cap. That cap is not account-wide.
Performance trade-off: Phrase search is cheap and predictable but may miss paraphrased clauses, scanned PDFs, tables, and unusual layouts. Compare output quality in a real sample run before claiming a quality/cost trade-off.

Measured once: One live gpt-6-luna review used the fictional sample lease. The following worksheet records its usage and shows a rate-based cost calculation; the provider billing dashboard was not used.

What the prototype cannot guarantee

A prompt is not a perfect security boundary. The app does not validate extracted claims against a complete legal corpus, inspect all possible prompt-injection variants, or provide professional review. Users should not upload sensitive unredacted leases and should not rely on this tool for legal decisions. The app offers no landlord contact, signing, payment, or application-submission tool.

Connecticut tenant resource: Department of Banking — Rental Security Deposits. The app links to this official source rather than restating legal conclusions as AI output.

07 · Limits and next steps

A useful first release should be honest about what it cannot do.

Current limitations

  • Only text-based PDF and TXT; scanned PDFs are not OCR processed.
  • Phrase matching can miss clauses and may return irrelevant sentences.
  • Housing data is a dated source-linked snapshot, not a live inventory feed.
  • No account system or secure document vault; checklist progress, saved listings, and notes stay in this browser and are not synced across devices.
  • No legal advice, landlord verification, or lease recommendation.
  • One live token measurement was completed on a fictional sample; cost is calculated from published OpenAI rates, not verified against a billing statement.
  • Cost estimates require renter-entered quotes and omit any charges the user does not enter.

Next month

  • Test with a wider range of redacted sample leases.
  • Add clause-level evaluation and quote verification.
  • Improve English-first instructions and preserve source text in every explanation.
  • Build a user-controlled reminder export.
  • Have a housing counselor review the safety boundaries.
  • Explore listing links through permitted official channels, with source and update date.

Development priorities

First: measure extraction quality and failure cases. Second: improve privacy controls and consent around model-provider transmission. Third: add only the local housing information that students need and can be kept current. A broader marketplace is a separate project.

Success criterion for a next version: a student can use the app to identify lease facts and questions more quickly than their current approach, without the app making unsupported claims or hiding uncertainty.
